# EchoTrace — The Missing Evidence

> **Advanced / Insane DFIR & Forensic Engineering Training Scenario**

**EchoTrace — The Missing Evidence** is a complete fictional enterprise incident-response and digital-forensics training scenario focused on catastrophic system outages, suspected AI-assisted targeted activity, destructive data loss, failed backup assumptions, and evidence-driven recovery.

The analyst is not asked merely to identify an attacker. The mission is to determine **what failed, what was targeted, what data can be recovered, what evidence supports automation or AI-assisted behavior, whether backups are trustworthy, and how the organization can safely restore operations without destroying forensic evidence.**

## Training purpose

EchoTrace is designed to train senior technicians in:

- digital evidence acquisition and preservation;
- Windows and server forensics;
- storage and file-system analysis;
- network and identity correlation;
- outage timeline engineering;
- backup and recovery validation;
- data-loss scoping;
- detection of automated or machine-generated operational patterns;
- incident containment;
- forensic recovery engineering;
- business continuity and disaster recovery;
- evidence-based executive reporting.

## Safety and authenticity

All organizations, people, systems, data, domains, IP addresses, events, and evidence are fictional or synthetically generated for authorized cybersecurity education.

No real customer data, production credentials, executable malware, destructive payloads, or operational intrusion tooling is included.

Claims of “AI-assisted” activity must be proven from supplied evidence. Students must not assume AI involvement simply because the scenario mentions it.

## Core incident

A fictional enterprise experiences cascading service failures across identity, application, storage, and backup infrastructure. Critical datasets become unavailable. Management believes backups exist, but restoration attempts reveal missing restore points and inconsistent catalog records.

At the same time, telemetry shows a sequence of highly coordinated actions occurring faster and more consistently than the organization's normal administrative baseline. Investigators must determine whether the evidence supports scripted automation, AI-assisted targeting, compromised orchestration, legitimate automation, or another explanation.

The investigation must answer four business-critical questions:

1. Why did the systems fail?
2. What data was actually lost or remains recoverable?
3. Can any backup be trusted for restoration?
4. What can the evidence prove about the coordinated activity?
